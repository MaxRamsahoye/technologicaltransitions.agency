The Complex Emergence Model

An elaboration of Schmachtenberger’s framework of civilisational structures: a modified version, called the complex emergence model for differentiation, that in effect doubles the number of structures, to eight.

  1. Core

    Tech Stack

    Infrastructure

    Technological instruments and affordances, productive (and reproductive) forces and material conditions.

    Political Economy

    Interstructure

    The institutional complex for decision-making (governance) and resource-allocation (economy).

    Cultural Psychology

    Superstructure

    Ideologies, worldviews, belief and value-systems, symbols, memetics and narratives.

    • Evolutionary Psychology

      Substructure

      Universal, innate cognitive-affective predispositions and capacities, which are expressed or suppressed through interaction primarily with the superstructure (and other structures).

  2. Interfaces‘Second Nature’

    Cultural-Evolutionary Biology

    Biostructure

    The physiological, genetic, somatic and neurobiological substrate of human populations, as altered and conditioned by cultural practices, environmental feedback and technology.

    Cultural-Evolutionary Ecology

    Ecostructure

    The biophysical environment, resource reservoirs, ecosystem dynamics and planetary boundaries within which human civilisations are embedded and intra-acting.

  3. Transecting

    System Dynamics

    Metastructure

    The cybernetic, game-theoretic and evolutionary mechanisms operational within a civilisation.

    • Cybernetic

      Goal-optimisation (‘teleology’), error-minimisation and feedback loops.

    • Game-Theoretic

      Incentive structures, rational strategising and agent-modelling.

    • Evolutionary

      Selection processes, competitive pressures and niche construction.

    Developmental Process

    Macrostructure

    The process by which a civilisation elaborates itself and changes across macrohistorical time.

    • States & Transitions

      The critical thresholds, ‘bifurcation points’ and ‘phase transitions’ where systems change, complexify and collapse.

    • Constraints & Affordances

      The hard boundaries, limiting factors and ‘action potentials’ generated at each stage of civilisational development.

    • Problems, Solutions & Externalities

      The historical process by which solutions to existing problems generate novel problems and unintended side-effects.
