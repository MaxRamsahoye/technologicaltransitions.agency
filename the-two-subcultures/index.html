A Critical Evolutionary Game-Theory Model of Major Technological Transitions

    Max Ramsahoye

    A Critical Evolutionary Game-Theory Model of Major Technological Transitions

    The civilisation-wide diffusion of a new tech stack is not predetermined by competitive pressures, but is enacted by the technocratic elite’s response to them

    Overview

    Our advanced, global civilisation is the result of a progressive series of technological revolutions – agricultural, industrial and digital – each fundamentally changing the political economy and cultural psychology of human populations, affording new strategic advantages and reshaping the fitness landscape of civilisation as a whole.

    So far civilisation has undergone three such major technological transitions. We are now in the midst of the fourth (and likely final): the Intelligence Revolution. The underlying structural dynamic driving it – military-economic adaptation, competitive pressures, multi-polar traps, arms races – is continuous with all preceding transitions. However, as an exponential technology, AI could generate negative externalities magnitudes more powerful and catastrophic, potentially even existential.

    In this article, this foundational civilisational problem is revisited by drawing on the literature of science and technology studies, in concert with case studies of previous technological revolutions throughout history. Principally, the article proposes a critical evolutionary game-theory model of major technological transitions: as structurally driven by global competitive pressures and also agentially enacted by the local decision-making of the power-elite. Ultimately, it is an open question whether pro-human intentions can prevail over the agents and dynamics of the current world-system.

    As E.P Thompson put it: “If Man is ever obliterated from the earth by means of his own armaments, there will be no simple answer to the question: Did he fall, or was he pushed?”.
